# Scott Johnston sources for scheduler improvements

Reviewed: 2026-10-03, Australia/Sydney.
Purpose: support the proposed scheduler reliability design, especially urban
Vietnamese trail runners. This is a curated source review, not a replacement
for the book or a claim that every podcast has been listened to.

## Evidence status

Article findings below come from inspected primary text. Podcast entries come
from publisher descriptions and chapter guides; their timestamps identify where
to listen, not verified quotations or approved numerical rules. Evoke team
advice is attributed to the team unless Scott is explicitly the author/speaker.
Forum responses have narrow athlete-specific context. Reader anecdotes and
third-party AI summaries are excluded as doctrine.

## Articles to use first

### S1. Muscular Endurance: All You Need to Know

[Scott Johnston, Evoke, 2022-10-06](https://evokeendurance.com/resources/muscular-endurance-all-you-need-to-know/).
Inspected sections: caveats, timing, gym protocol and progression.

Scott explicitly acknowledges evolution from earlier writing. He distinguishes
mountain-runner ME timing from mountaineering timing, keeps aerobic work central,
and adjusts introductory loading to experience and recovery. His gym protocol
completes each exercise's sets before changing exercises. Its use of “circuit”
later does not justify the app's blanket prohibition on straight sets.

Application: select a named, sport-appropriate protocol with prerequisites and
progression history. Do not make every runner follow an identical ME phase or
copy elite loading. The source's rollback after missed ME sessions belongs to
that progression, not every kind of training.

### S2. Updated Thinking on Aerobic Assessment for Mountain Athletes

[Scott Johnston, Evoke, 2022-10-26](https://evokeendurance.com/resources/our-latest-thinking-on-aerobic-assessment-for-the-mountain-athlete/).
Inspected threshold-testing and interpretation sections.

The article assigns AeT to the top of Zone 2 and AnT to the top of Zone 3. Its
threshold-spread guideline is described as a coaching observation, not a
scientifically derived universal formula.

Application: repair contradictory threshold terminology in the seed; preserve
measurement provenance. Do not use an estimated threshold pair to give a firm
physiological classification. Define the exact percentage denominator rather
than mixing the article's convention with the app's current tier formula.
Changing tier anchors is outside this reliability change without separate review.

### S3. Capacity vs Utilization Training: A Deep Dive

[Scott Johnston, Evoke, 2022-09-25](https://evokeendurance.com/resources/capacity-vs-utilization-training/).
Inspected conceptual introduction.

The article separates developing underlying abilities from combining those
abilities in event-like workouts, and cautions against overemphasizing the latter.

Application (design inference): flat aerobic weekdays can serve a trail runner's
preparation; every workout need not imitate the race. Weekend mountain access
should support appropriate specificity without turning every weekend into a race
simulation. Fitness, phase and recovery still determine the prescription.

### S4. Treadmill Season

[Scott Johnston, Evoke, 2022-12-07](https://evokeendurance.com/resources/treadmill-season/).
Inspected introduction and attribution boundary.

Scott explains why treadmill speed/grade accuracy matters for controlled work;
the later installation account is a reader contribution. The article also
distinguishes ordinary treadmills from steeper incline trainers.

Application: machine capability is an input, not an assumption. Indoor ascent
is an estimate; device-specific discrepancies must not become a global
correction factor. Do not prescribe an elite incline-trainer session on an
ordinary machine by simply reducing its grade and claiming equivalence.

### S5. Training Principles for the Uphill Athlete

[Scott Johnston, published book excerpt, TrainingPeaks](https://www.trainingpeaks.com/blog/training-for-the-uphill-athlete-continuity-gradualness-and-modulation/).
Published 2019-05-23; page lists update 2026-06-05. Inspected in the preceding
research pass; included here as the main book-to-design connection.

Application: base progression on training actually absorbed, account for
interruptions and allow recovery. A distance-preservation fix must not undo a
justified reduction. The excerpt does not establish the app's per-tier long-run
caps or an unconditional weekly increase.

### S6. Welcome to your Custom Plan

[Evoke team guidance; no Scott byline](https://evokeendurance.com/custom-plan-welcome/).
Publication date not shown in inspected result.

The guidance discourages cramming missed work into later days and gives
adjustment/recovery rules for its custom plans.

Application: supports testing explicit missed sessions separately from unlogged
ones. Treat the page's numeric rules as contextual team guidance, not universal
book rules or a substitute for missing readiness information.

### S7. Chapter 5: Application — Scott's forum response

[Official archived discussion](https://evokeendurance.com/forums/topic/chapter-5-application/).
Inspected question and Scott's reply; exact publication date not established.

Scott explains that an anecdote about repeatable daily mileage illustrates
capacity rather than prescribing how to develop it; modulation still matters.

Application: avoid turning a recovery heuristic into a rigid requirement that
every long run must be repeatable the next morning. Context-specific forum
answers require the question alongside the answer before reuse.

## Implications for the proposed design

1. Resolve the ME protocol contradiction using S1; preserve protocol-specific
   differences rather than blend incompatible sets, rests and progressions.
2. Add sport, preparation history and phase to the rule register. A short race
   runway does not by itself justify an advanced workout.
3. Use S2 for zone terminology; label threshold-derived decisions according to
   source reliability. Separate tier estimation from intensity eligibility.
4. Keep accounting multidimensional: movement time, running/hiking distance,
   ascent and strength/ME. A time-based model is an engineering interpretation
   for this app; these articles do not validate every proposed denominator.
5. Check indoor capability and outdoor access before selecting a session. The
   four Vietnam cases should test both constraints and recovery feasibility.
6. Distinguish justified adaptation from arithmetic loss. An explicit missed
   week and a week with no logs must not become interchangeable evidence.
7. Keep doctrine acceptance separate from numeric fixture acceptance. A passing
   weekly-distance gate does not establish a valid training stimulus.

## What this search did not establish

- No inspected Scott source established universal 33%/30% tier caps or a 50%
  combined weekend cap. This is absence of confirmation in this search, not
  proof that Scott has never discussed those figures.
- No reviewed podcast transcript establishes a blanket requirement to sprint
  on a standard treadmill or a universal conversion from steep incline trainers.
- Exact book-page confirmation remains necessary for rules attributed specifically
  to the book where the inspected author articles differ or evolve.
- The current app's weekly-volume bands remain regression gates; this review
  neither changes them nor treats them as physiological laws.

Next design work: use S1–S7 in the source register, verify the two priority podcast
segments, and settle the remaining contextual policy decisions before writing
implementation steps. No knowledge ingestion, prompt update or label movement
was performed as part of this research.
