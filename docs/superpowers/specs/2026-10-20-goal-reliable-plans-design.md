# Goal-reliable plans: from "plausible schedule" to "plan that earns the goal"

Status: draft for owner approval, 2026-10-20. Builds on #149–#154 (plan_lint, race_readiness, plan_adequacy, week_layout).

## Intent

The owner's bar: a plan generated by our system, with the athlete's full context available, is **reliable to reach the athlete's goal** — B for sure, A on their best day — or the system says plainly that it is not and what would change that.

No system can promise a finish time. What we can promise, and must be able to *prove with evidence*, is:

1. **Fit**: the plan is built from the goal's physical demands, not from a generic template.
2. **Feasibility honesty**: if the athlete's days, time, history or weeks cannot support the goal, we say so before they train, and plan to the best feasible target.
3. **Safety**: no week breaks the doctrine's hard rules (the lint), with or without the LLM.
4. **Monitoring**: every week we measure, from real data, whether the athlete is on the path to B and A, and adapt training or the goal.
5. **A claim we can defend**: a reliability scorecard (below) that passes on a fixed scenario set, plus live tracking after launch.

## Where we are (verified in code, 2026-10-20)

- The goal reaches the generator as one sentence (`Goal: Finish in 14h40m`, `plan_generator.py` ~1156) and one race-pace string on Zone 4 sessions (~815, ~937). Course steepness is a prompt line. Nothing converts a goal into required pace, climbing rate, time on feet or long-run gradient.
- `PacingCalculator.solve_base_pace` can already invert a target time to the flat pace the athlete must hold on a given course.
- Checks that exist and are on branch `feat/goal-plan-support` (PR #154, not yet on main): structural lint with one corrective retry; race readiness in flat-equivalent km; goal adequacy (goals never assume more improvement than the plan delivers); a rule-based fallback that obeys the athlete's days.
- Missing: goal-derived demands, feasibility by days/time, real-data tracking, the A gate, and a reliability scorecard.

## Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

Goals
- Every generated plan carries a **demand spec** (what the goal needs) and a **coverage report** (what the plan delivers), visible to the athlete and checked by code.
- An honest **feasibility verdict** per goal: `supported`, `stretch` (supported only with changes), `unsupported` (plan to the best feasible target, show the gap).
- Weekly **path status** for B and A from real watch data.
- A **reliability scorecard** run in CI on fixed scenarios, with pass thresholds.

Non-goals
- Promising a finish time or a guaranteed outcome.
- New training philosophy: the book rules (`plan_rules`, `[UA-Rn]`) stay the source of truth.
- Strava or Garmin integrations (blocked, see memory); COROS and Apple Health data only.

Anti-goals (a failure even if it "works")
- Raising load to chase A when recovery data says no.
- Silently shrinking the goal. Every change to a goal is shown and confirmed by the athlete.
- Presenting low-confidence tracking as fact.

## Design

### 1. Goal demands (new `services/goal_demands.py`, pure)
For each of A/B/C and for the athlete's own target: from the course profile (GPX checkpoints if curated, else synthetic), `solve_base_pace`, and the book's benchmarks, compute:
- required flat-equivalent pace and the share of the race above AeT effort;
- required climbing rate (m D+/h) and descending load;
- time on feet and the longest continuous effort the plan must contain;
- peak-week target in flat-equivalent km and in hours (book: 50K 80–88 km, 100K 120–130 km), and the weekly-time **capacity ceiling** for the athlete's days per week (tier caps).
Output is a small dataclass, deterministic, unit-tested on the VHT 50K and 100K courses.

### 2. Goal-shaped generation
- The demand spec is injected into the generation context as a short block (required pace, climb rate, long-run targets), replacing the one-line goal sentence. One prompt-variable change, so a new Langfuse `plan_generation` version, staged first.
- Rule-based fallback uses the same spec for its long-run and climbing targets.
- New lint codes: `LONG_RUN_GRADIENT` (race-specific long runs reach the course's required climbing rate by the Specific period), `TIME_ON_FEET` (longest session reaches the book fraction of expected race time, within the 4–5 h cap), `SPECIFICITY_LATE` (race-pace and climbing work present in the last build weeks).

### 3. Feasibility by capacity (new `services/goal_feasibility.py`)
Required peak vs what the athlete's days, tier caps and weeks left can hold, plus the improvement the plan can deliver (`plan_adequacy`). Verdict per goal with a plain-language reason and levers (more days, double sessions, more weeks, a shorter race, finish-only). Example from this project: 3 days/week holds ≈3.7 h for a recreational runner, a 100K peak needs ≈11 h, so verdict `unsupported` with the levers listed. Plan trains to the best feasible target; the athlete's goal stays visible as a stretch. Bilingual copy (EN/VI, per the Vietnamese copy skill).

### 4. Tracking: the aerobic-speed index (new `services/aerobic_speed.py`, table `fitness_checkpoints`)
Weekly median grade-adjusted pace at AeT-band heart rate from synced runs (≥30 min, ≥3 qualifying runs, no intervals). Compared with the improvement path (0.25%/week) and with the pace each goal requires. Output `ahead | on | behind` for B and A with tolerance and hysteresis, and a confidence level (low when few samples or no watch). Feeds a new anchor into `goal_anchors` so the weekly goal reassess uses training evidence. Optional checkpoint sessions on recovery weeks raise confidence.

### 5. A gate
B is the contract. A add-ons (extra specific climbing, goal-pace blocks, volume toward the benchmark) are layered on only while all hold: aerobic-speed on or ahead of the A line, recovery signals fine (HRV/resting HR), adherence ≥80%, adequacy OK. Gate off means add-ons off; B is untouched. Add-ons obey the same lint.

### 6. Reliability scorecard (the claim)
`scripts/plan_reliability.py` runs the full pipeline on a fixed scenario matrix (athlete × race × goal: 4 profiles × 50K/100K × A/B/own target, plus edge cases: injury return, 3-day athlete, short runway) with the model mocked by recorded responses and with the rule-based fallback. Per scenario it checks:
- lint clean on hard rules; readiness at or above the floor at peak;
- demand coverage (long-run gradient, time on feet, specificity) met or an explicit verdict that it cannot be;
- feasibility verdict consistent with capacity (a 3-day 100K must not read `supported`);
- goals ordered A < B < C and never beyond what the plan supports.
Pass thresholds are set in CI. We state the claim only when the matrix passes and the live checks below are done. Live: after deploy, throwaway plans on prod with the real model for retry hit-rate and coach review; then, with real users, path status vs outcomes (P5).

## What is left to the builder
File layout inside `services/`, dataclass names, exact tolerance bands and hysteresis constants (start conservative, document), copy wording (EN first, VI via the copy skill), and the Alembic revision for `fitness_checkpoints` (dual schema: `init_db` plus a hand-written migration).

## Calls I made (owner can overrule)
- Verdict names `supported | stretch | unsupported`.
- Time-on-feet target as a fraction of expected race time, capped at the tier/book session limit, constants taken from the book extraction in `plan_rules`.
- The scorecard uses recorded model responses so CI never spends Gemini budget.
- Order: land #154 → goal demands + feasibility (no schema) → scorecard → tracking (schema) → A gate.

## Open risks
Noisy aerobic-speed signal (heat, terrain, HR drift): medians, minimum samples, hysteresis, never raise intensity from one data point. Few athletes have both results and training data, so calibration of "on path" thresholds is mostly logic tests early on. Prompt changes need the golden scheduler eval before and after.
